Florence was the second stop of our Italy trip in October 2024, between Naples and Venice. We were there on 10 and 11 October.

A very beautiful city, very pleasant to walk in. And a very good vintage shop.

## Florence overview

**When:** 10 and 11 October 2024.

**How long we stayed:** two days.

**How long I would stay:** three to four days. My only regret is not having spent more time there.

## Where to stay in Florence

We stayed in an Airbnb a little outside the centre, and it was really lovely.

## Best things to do in Florence

### Sunrise at Piazzale Michelangelo

The best moment of our stay. We went up for sunrise, with the view over the whole city, and we even got a rainbow.

![Piazzale Michelangelo](/images/journal/florence/piazzale-evening.jpg)

![Photographing the view over Florence](/images/journal/florence/photographing-duomo.jpg)

**Good to know:** Piazzale Michelangelo is free and open at all hours. From the Ponte Vecchio, count about twenty minutes on foot, uphill, or take bus 12 or 13. On the way up, the Rose Garden and the San Niccolò steps give you the same view with fewer people. At sunrise you have it almost to yourself.

### The Duomo

Santa Maria del Fiore and Brunelleschi's dome, the biggest masonry dome ever built.

**Good to know:** entering the cathedral is free, but you queue. Everything else, the dome climb, Giotto's bell tower, the baptistery, the crypt and the museum, works with a combined pass from the Opera del Duomo. The dome climb needs a reserved time slot, and slots go fast: book online well ahead.

### The Ponte Vecchio

The old bridge lined with goldsmiths' shops, the only bridge in Florence that survived the war.

### The Boboli Gardens

The Renaissance gardens behind the Pitti Palace, with terraces, fountains and views over the city.

**Good to know:** the gardens are paid, and the ticket is usually combined with the Pitti Palace. Allow one to two hours: it climbs, and the best views are at the top.

### Vintage shopping

Florence has a very good vintage scene, and one shop in particular is worth the trip.

## How many days in Florence?

Three to four days. With two, I left with the feeling of having rushed it.

## Florence FAQ

**How many days in Florence?**
Three to four days.

**When should you go up to Piazzale Michelangelo?**
At sunrise. The view over the whole city is the same, and you have the place almost to yourself.

**Do you need to book for the Duomo?**
The cathedral itself is free, but the dome climb needs a combined ticket with a reserved time slot, booked in advance.

**Where to stay in Florence?**
We stayed in an Airbnb slightly outside the centre, which was quieter and cheaper.
