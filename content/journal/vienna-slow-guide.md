Vienna was one of the last stops of my Interrail journey this summer, twelve stops across Europe that ended in Prague. I arrived by train from Graz, over the Semmering, and stayed four nights.

What stays with me is the scale of it: the architecture is immense. I spent most of my time walking through the city, looking for the light.

## Where I stayed

I stayed in two hotels in Vienna, Altstadt Vienna and Hotel Rathaus Wein & Design. Each one gets its own story in the journal.

## Where I ate

Three places, three different sides of Viennese cooking, plus one easy option.

### Beisl in der Sigmundsgasse

Sigmundsgasse 12, Spittelberg, 7th district

A tiny, no-frills place near Altstadt, run by an older couple. It has been open since 1982, with the same host all along, which is rare in the neighbourhood.

It is really small: two rooms, one for about ten people and one for about twenty. Book a table.

The house rule is simple: the Wiener schnitzel is made from pork and fried in a pan, never in a deep fryer. The beef roulade is the other dish they are known for, and there are always seasonal dishes and a few vegetarian ones.

The street itself is worth the walk. Sigmundsgasse is part of Spittelberg, a whole ensemble of Biedermeier houses protected as a group.

### Amerlingbeisl

Stiftgasse 8, Spittelberg, 7th district

The reason to go is the inner courtyard, with a vine growing over the whole thing. Go on a warm evening and sit outside under the leaves.

The house dates from 1700, and the painter Friedrich Amerling was born there in 1803. In the 1970s, neighbours, artists and students occupied the empty building to save it from demolition. It became a cultural centre, and the beisl has been open inside it since 1980.

The menu is simple Viennese cooking that changes with the seasons, with a weekly menu. Their speciality is an organic veal schnitzel, pan-fried.

### Gasthaus Zu den 3 Hacken

Singerstraße 28, 1st district

The classic address for a schnitzel, in one of the oldest inns in Vienna. Low vaulted rooms, old wooden floors and wooden benches.

In the 19th century it was a favourite of Franz Schubert and his circle of friends, and one of the rooms is named after him. The playwright Johann Nestroy even mentioned it in one of his songs.

The house was restored and reopened in 2021, and the cooking is traditional Viennese: Wiener schnitzel, onion roast beef, apricot dumplings for dessert.

![Wiener schnitzel with potato salad at Zu den 3 Hacken](/images/journal/vienna/3-hacken-schnitzel.jpg)

### Option: Café Eiles

Josefstädter Straße 2, 8th district

A good option that is not expensive. This is where I had my goulash, served with a fried egg on top.

Café Eiles is one of the oldest coffee houses in the city: there has been a café here since 1838, under the Eiles name since 1901. It is open every day until midnight, and it is close to the Rathaus.

One honest note: the service really depends on who you get.

![Goulash with a fried egg at Café Eiles](/images/journal/vienna/cafe-eiles-goulash.jpg)

## A few tips

- Walk. Most of what I loved in Vienna, I found between two addresses.
- Go out early. The city is quiet, and the light on the facades is at its best.
