Five cameras, each with its own job. Here is what each one is, and why it is in my bag.

## Ricoh GR III: my main camera

**What I use it for:** it is my main body, the camera I reach for first.

**What it is:** a pocket compact built around a large 24.2MP APS-C sensor, with a fixed 18.3mm f/2.8 lens, the equivalent of a 28mm. It has 3-axis shake reduction, which helps with slower shutter speeds handheld, a macro mode for close details, and crop modes to 35mm and 50mm if you need them.

## Pentax 17: my main film camera

**What I use it for:** when I shoot film, this is the one.

**What it is:** a half-frame camera for standard 35mm film. Each frame is 17 x 24mm, so you get about twice as many photos per roll, around 72 on a 36-exposure roll. Half-frame images are naturally vertical.

**The lens:** a fixed 25mm f/3.5, the equivalent of a 37mm, with Pentax HD coating.

**How it works:** no screen, no menus. You wind the film with a lever and focus with six zones, from macro at 0.25m up to infinity, marked with little icons you can see through the viewfinder.

## Insta360 Luna Ultra: filming and vlogging

**What I use it for:** to film and to vlog.

**What it is:** a pocket camera with a built-in 3-axis gimbal and two lenses: a main Leica lens on a 1-inch sensor, up to 8K, and a telephoto on a 1/1.3-inch sensor, with up to 12x zoom.

**Why it is practical for vlogging:** AI tracking keeps you in the frame, and its 2-inch touchscreen detaches to work as a wireless remote.

## Insta360 Ace Pro 2: the all-terrain one

**What I use it for:** the all-terrain side of things.

**What it is:** an action camera co-engineered with Leica, with a 1/1.3-inch sensor, up to 8K30 and 4K60 video, and a very wide 157° field of view.

**Why it goes anywhere:** it is waterproof to 12 metres straight out of the box, and its 2.5-inch touchscreen flips up so you can frame yourself.

## DJI Lito 1: my drone

**What I use it for:** aerial shots on my travels. It was my first drone, and it is really beginner friendly.

**What it is:** a folding drone under 250 grams, in the C0 class in Europe, with a 48MP 1/2-inch sensor on a 3-axis gimbal, obstacle sensing all around, and up to 36 minutes of flight with the standard battery.

**Good to know:** the optional Plus battery gives longer flights but takes the drone over 250 grams, which can change the rules that apply to you. Check the regulations where you fly.

## The fun extras

Besides these five, I also bring a few gadget cameras just for fun: a Canon film compact, a NiceFlex toy camera and two tiny Kodak keychain cameras, plus a few rolls of Kodak film.

*I work with Ricoh, Pentax and Insta360. What I carry, and why, is my own choice.*
