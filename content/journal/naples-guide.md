Naples was the first stop of our Italy trip in October 2024, before Florence and Venice. We were there on 8 and 9 October.

Naples is dirty and intimidating. It is also very cool for street photography.

## Naples overview

**When:** 8 and 9 October 2024.

**How long we stayed:** two days, and it was not enough.

**How long I would stay:** three days, I think, but I am not sure. I did not get to see enough of the city.

## Our arrival: four hours late, in a storm

Our flight was four hours late. Instead of landing at 2pm, we arrived at 6pm, in the middle of a storm. That first evening was written off.

**Good to know:** from the airport, the Alibus shuttle goes to the central station and the port for about €5, in 15 to 35 minutes. A taxi is a fixed fare, around €25.

## The pizza that was not the one

We had a pizza. It was not very good, and we were treated like dirt on top of it: seated in a cold corner of the restaurant with no phone signal, and a very long wait.

![Pizza in Naples](/images/journal/naples/pizza.jpg)

**What I would do differently:** book, or go to one of the pizzerias people actually queue for, and go early. In the city where pizza was invented, a bad pizza feels like a waste of a meal.

## The morning that saved Naples for me

The next morning we got up early, took a taxi up to the heights of the city, and walked all the way back down to the station through the alleys. That was much nicer.

![Naples seen from the heights in the morning](/images/journal/naples/view-from-heights.jpg)

![At the viewpoint above Naples](/images/journal/naples/viewpoint-portrait.jpg)

**Good to know:** the heights are the Vomero hill. You do not need a taxi: three funiculars go up from the city centre, and they are included in the normal transport ticket, around €1.50 to €1.80 for 90 minutes. At the top, Castel Sant'Elmo has the best view over Naples, the bay and Vesuvius, for about €5, and the Certosa di San Martino sits right next to it.

![Walking through the cloister](/images/journal/naples/cloister-portrait.jpg)

## Walking down through the alleys

This is where Naples becomes something else. The alleys are still full of workshops and small trades, and that is my photo spot in this city.

![A cat in a Naples alley](/images/journal/naples/cat-flowerpot.jpg)

![A yellow car in the street](/images/journal/naples/yellow-car.jpg)

![A narrow street in Naples](/images/journal/naples/narrow-street.jpg)

**Good to know:** the spine of the old town is Spaccanapoli, the straight street that cuts the historic centre in two. Walk it first thing in the morning, before the scooters take over.

![An archway into a courtyard](/images/journal/naples/archway.jpg)

![Green shutters in Naples](/images/journal/naples/green-shutters.jpg)

Just off Spaccanapoli, Via San Gregorio Armeno is the street of the nativity figure workshops, open all year. The alleys around it are full of small trades: baskets, ceramics, food stalls.

![A basket and vegetable shop](/images/journal/naples/basket-shop.jpg)

![Baskets hanging in the street](/images/journal/naples/basket-shop-2.jpg)

## Getting to Florence

From Naples, we took the train to Florence. Fast trains do it in about three hours, from Napoli Centrale to Firenze Santa Maria Novella.

## How many days in Naples?

Three days, I think. With two, and a storm, I did not see enough.

## Naples FAQ

**Is Naples worth visiting?**
Yes, especially if you photograph. It is dirty and intimidating, and it is one of the best cities I know for street photography.

**How many days in Naples?**
Three days, to have time for the centre, the heights and a day trip.

**What is the best area for photography in Naples?**
The alleys of the old town, which are still full of workshops and small trades.

**How do you get from the airport to Naples?**
The Alibus shuttle to the central station, for about €5.

**How do you get up to the top of Naples?**
By funicular from the centre, included in the normal transport ticket, to the Vomero hill and Castel Sant'Elmo.
