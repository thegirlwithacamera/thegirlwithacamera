## What I used before

Before the Luna Ultra, I vlogged with the DJI Osmo Pocket 3.

## How I use it

- **Cinematic travel vlogs,** on the go.
- **Hotel videos,** for the stays I film.

## Insta360 Luna Ultra: the key specs

**Two Leica lenses.** The main camera uses a Leica Summicron lens on a 1-inch sensor, at f/1.8, the equivalent of a 20mm. A second telephoto camera sits next to it, with a 1/1.3-inch sensor at f/2.0, the equivalent of a 60mm.

**Video.** Up to 8K at 30 frames per second, with Dolby Vision and 10-bit I-Log for colour grading.

**Stabilisation.** A 3-axis mechanical gimbal, combined with electronic stabilisation.

**Weight.** Just over 200 grams.

**Leica looks.** Three Leica colour profiles, Leica Natural, Leica Vivid and Leica Chrome, plus cinematic filters.

## What I love about the Luna Ultra

### 1. The detachable screen

My favourite feature. The 2-inch OLED touchscreen comes off the camera and becomes a wireless remote, with a live view of what the lenses see, from up to 20 metres away. You can leave the camera on a tripod, frame yourself, zoom and start recording from a distance. The remote even has its own microphone.

### 2. The 12x zoom

Five focal lengths, up to 12x zoom. Reviewers agree that quality stays nearly lossless up to 6x, thanks to the telephoto lens, and that beyond that the camera crops into the sensor.

## Any downsides?

Honestly, none for me. In my use, the Luna Ultra is perfect.

To be complete, here is what other reviews point out, so you know before you buy:

- **Heat on long takes.** In one test, the camera shut down after about 44 minutes in 4K at 120 fps, and after about 49 minutes in 8K at 24 fps.
- **Two batteries to charge,** one in the camera and one in the detachable remote.
- **The switch between the two lenses** around 3x zoom can sometimes show a slight colour difference.

## My favourite setting

**Leica Vivid.** It is the profile I use.
