We were in Palermo from 23 to 27 March 2026, with my boyfriend, with a day trip to Cefalù in the middle.

Palermo reminded me of Naples. I liked it a lot, especially the markets.

## Palermo overview

**When:** 23 to 27 March 2026.

**How long we stayed:** five days, with one day in Cefalù.

**Our rhythm:** walk, photograph, eat and drink, on repeat.

**What I would do differently:** do Sicily as a ten-day road trip and go all the way round the island.

## Best things to do in Palermo

### The markets

This is what I liked most in Palermo, and the **Mercato del Capo** is the one to go to.

**Good to know:** the Capo runs along Via Porta Carini, in the Capo quarter, and works every morning, best before noon. Palermo's other historic markets are Ballarò, the biggest and the most working-class, and the Vucciria, quiet by day and a drinking spot at night.

### Walking and looking up

The old town is layered: baroque facades, peeling palaces, laundry, and light coming down into the alleys.

![Looking up in the old town](/images/journal/palermo/looking-up.jpg)

### Breakfast at Ideal Caffè Stagnitta

Discesa dei Giudici 42, just off Via Vittorio Emanuele. A coffee roaster since 1922, where we had breakfast: brioche and pistachio coffee.

![Brioche and pistachio coffee at Ideal Caffè Stagnitta](/images/journal/palermo/granita-brioche.jpg)

![Breakfast, with the camera on the table](/images/journal/palermo/breakfast-camera.jpg)

**Good to know:** they roast their own beans, and the espresso is one of the best in the city. Open 7am to 7pm, closed on Sundays.

### Eating your way through the rest of the day

Something fried at a market stall at noon, a pizza in the evening.

**Good to know:** Palermo is a street food city. The classics are panelle (chickpea fritters), arancine (the rice balls, feminine here, unlike in Catania), sfincione (the local spongy pizza) and, for the brave, pane con la milza, the spleen sandwich.

### Nni Franco U Vastiddaru, for a sandwich

Via Vittorio Emanuele 102, by the fountain. A Palermo street food institution, with tables outside. I had ham, cheese and salad on bread.

![Ham, cheese and salad on bread](/images/journal/palermo/ham-rocket.jpg)

**Good to know:** open from 9am to 11pm or later. It is table service, with a small cover charge per person, and the place is busy, so the wait can be long when it fills up.

### Timilia, for pizza

Via Maqueda 221, right on the main pedestrian street. Surprisingly delicious for such a touristy street.

![Pizza at Timilia](/images/journal/palermo/pizza-evening.jpg)

![Another pizza at Timilia](/images/journal/palermo/pizza-lunch.jpg)

**Good to know:** they are open all day, from 11:30am to midnight, later at the weekend, and they do a gluten-free pizza that people come back for. There is a table service charge, so do not be surprised by the bill.

## Where to drink: dal Barone

Via Alessandro Paternostro 87, in the Kalsa. A tiny natural wine bar, where you sit outside on the kerb with a glass and a board of cheese and cured meats.

![The front of dal Barone](/images/journal/palermo/dal-barone.jpg)

![Cheese and cured meats](/images/journal/palermo/charcuterie.jpg)

**Good to know:** the wines are Sicilian and natural, the owner knows every bottle, and it is open in the evening from 6pm, plus lunchtimes at the weekend. It is closed on Fridays.

## How many days in Palermo?

Five days felt right for the city and one day trip. But if I went back, I would do the whole island in ten days, by car.

## Palermo FAQ

Yes. It reminded me of Naples, and the markets are the best part.

**Which market should you go to in Palermo?**
The Mercato del Capo, in the morning.

**How many days in Palermo?**
Five days with a day trip, or better, ten days for the whole island by car.

Dal Barone, in the Kalsa, for natural Sicilian wine and a board of cheese and cured meats.
