Burano is the island of coloured houses, out in the Venetian lagoon. We went for the day from Venice, in October 2024, and it is one of the two places I photographed the most on this trip.

## How to get to Burano

Vaporetto line 12 from Fondamente Nove, on the north side of Venice, about 40 to 45 minutes, with a stop at Murano on the way.

**Good to know:** a single ticket is €9.50 and lasts 75 minutes, so it does not cover the round trip. For an island day the 24-hour pass at €25 pays for itself, since going to Murano and Burano and back is four boardings.

**Where the colours come from:** the island is a fishing island, and the story goes that the houses were painted in bright colours so the fishermen could recognise their own home coming back through the fog of the lagoon. Today you cannot repaint a house in any colour you like: you ask the commune, which tells you which colours are allowed for that house.

### The leaning bell tower

The campanile of the church of San Martino leans visibly, like a small Venetian Pisa.

### Lace

Burano has been a lace island for centuries, with its own needle lace technique. There is a lace museum on the main square, and the shops around it sell everything from real handmade pieces to machine-made souvenirs.

## How long do you need in Burano?

Half a day is enough to walk the whole island, but go early or stay late: between 11am and 4pm, the boats unload their crowds.

## Burano FAQ

**How do you get to Burano from Venice?**
Vaporetto line 12 from Fondamente Nove, about 40 to 45 minutes.

**How much does it cost to get to Burano?**
€9.50 for a single ticket valid 75 minutes, or €25 for the 24-hour pass, which is better for an island day.

**Why are the houses in Burano coloured?**
The tradition comes from the fishing families, and the colours are still regulated by the commune today.

**Is Burano worth it?**
Yes. With the alleys of Venice, it is what I photographed the most on this trip.
