On 6 August 2024, for my birthday, my boyfriend gave me his Ricoh GR III. I had not owned a camera for years. It is the camera that brought me back to photography.

Mine is the Diary Edition, and it lives up to its name: it is my own way of journaling.

Nothing. I had not had a camera for years.

## How I use it

For everything. It is my way of journaling.

## Ricoh GR III: the key specs

**Sensor and lens.** A 24MP APS-C sensor, with a fixed 28mm equivalent lens at f/2.8.

**Stabilisation and focus.** 3-axis image stabilisation, and phase detection autofocus.

**Screen.** A 3-inch touchscreen.

**Size.** It fits in a pocket.

## What makes the Diary Edition different

It is the same camera as the standard GR III, with three differences:

- **The finish.** A metallic warm grey body with a textured surface, a dark brown grip, and a natural silver ring around the lens.
- **The Negative Film image control.** A mode that recreates the look of prints made from colour negative film, with rich colours and a slightly faded tone.
- **The shutdown screen.** When you turn it off, the camera shows how many photos you took that day.

The Negative Film mode and the shutdown screen were later added to the standard GR III and GR IIIx through a firmware update.

## The downside

**A loose contact in the battery compartment.** Apparently it is a known issue on the Ricoh GR, but for me it has never been a real handicap.

## My favourite setting

**Snap Focus at 1.5m, for street photography.** You set a fixed focus distance in advance, and when you press the shutter button all the way down in one go, the camera takes the picture at that distance straight away, without waiting for the autofocus. I keep mine at 1.5 metres.

**Negative Film, for the look.** It is the image control I use.

## Who should buy the Ricoh GR III?

- **Shy people who want to start street photography.**
- **Not for viewfinder lovers.** There is no built-in viewfinder, you frame on the screen.
